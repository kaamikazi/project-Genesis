## Runtime edit during generation

Shortly after the pipeline started at commit `3f7b0eb` (clean), two runtime files were edited:

- `genesis/storage.py` gained a new `Store.diagnostics` method, which reads recent decision diagnostics for the observatory.
- `genesis/api.py` gained the `/api/runs/{id}` diagnostics field and the v0.5 evidence in `/api/experiments`.

The exact patch is `dirty-at-generation-genesis.patch`. Its effect on each step:

- **Calibration:** imported the committed code. Its process started before the edit.
- **Protocol freeze:** recorded `runtime_code_dirty: true` and listed the files.
- **P, T and later steps:** imported the edited `storage.py`. No experiment imports `api.py`, and no experiment calls `Store.diagnostics`.

**Audit:** `tools/audit_generation_patch_m5.py` re-ran every completed P and T world (40 worlds) from a clean worktree at `3f7b0eb` and compared the initial hash, every tick-record hash and the final state digest with the stored exports. Result: see `verification/generation-patch-audit.json` (all 40 identical). No evidence needed regenerating.

## Known defect affecting one condition

The beam policy does not check that its submission's admission reference is still current. In T/library only, overlapping cohorts share a library, and another agent's admission can supersede a researcher's reference while its candidate waits. The researcher then re-submits a rejected candidate on every tick. This happened in 7 of 30 researcher lifetimes in that condition: 209 rejected submissions in total, at most 66 in one lifetime.

These rejections spent no credits or energy, but they cost those agents their turns. The library condition is therefore handicapped, and its results are a lower bound for that design. No other condition shows a single stale-reference rejection. The defect was found after evaluation and **was not fixed in this release**: fixing it and re-running would change a policy after seeing evaluation results. It is scheduled for the follow-up.
