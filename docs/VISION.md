# Vision

**Can a society of persistent artificial agents accumulate, preserve and improve useful capabilities through interaction, invention and knowledge transmission?**

GENESIS is meant to grow into a long-running world where inhabitants with limited views and persistent histories gather, trade, build tools that measurably work, teach each other, form institutions, and pass knowledge to later generations. Every such claim should be backed by a world transition, an artifact or a measurement you can inspect.

## The principles that do not change

- **Evidence over narrative.** A statement, a belief, an attempted action, an accepted action, an observed outcome, a verified artifact and an analyst's interpretation are different things, and the system keeps them apart.
- **Mechanical authority.** One engine owns the world. Controllers only propose.
- **No secret omniscience.** Controllers see restricted, local observations.
- **Honest experiments.** Every attempt is recorded, including failed ones, and nothing is cherry-picked.
- **No inflated language.** GENESIS does not claim consciousness, AGI, biological evolution or emergence. Changes to stored memory and skills are kept distinct from changes to model weights. No weights change at all.

## Where it stands (v0.2.0)

A small, complete foundation runs fully offline on a laptop:

- two settlements and eight inhabitants
- conserved resources
- a bounded program language with an independent evaluator
- a search controller that invents procedures and then improves them through verified revisions during the run
- library-based transmission with traceable adoption
- creator-removal experiments with matched controls
- transactional persistence with replay, fork and export
- a visual observatory

**What it is not yet:** a civilization. The inhabitants follow designed rules, the "invention" is a hill-climb in a 16-program space, and social behaviour is scripted. Nothing has emerged. [IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS](IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md) lists exactly what exists.

## The road from here

[ROADMAP](ROADMAP.md) sets out the dependency order:

1. A larger population.
2. LLM controllers under budgets.
3. Teaching.
4. Settlement institutions and shared infrastructure.
5. Succession with new agents and inheritance packages.
6. Cumulative improvement across generations.
7. Multiple settlements with richer geography.
8. Human visitors acting through the same validated contracts.
9. Generalisation across unfamiliar worlds.

The long-term ambition stays enormous: a world in which useful knowledge accumulates beyond any one inhabitant's lifetime, with proof.
